§2 Sunday — Open-Air or Premier?weighed against the forecast and the family, not just the romance of it

Blue Ridge Railway genuinely offers both, so this is a real choice: Open-Air Coach (bench seating facing out, large open windows, no climate control, all ages) or Premier (climate-controlled, reclining seats, 18+ only, quiet, unlimited drinks, catered snack). You can't blend them on one ride.

What the forecast actually says

Your source's hour-by-hour estimate for Jul 26: 9–11am clear/light clouds, 68–74°F, low risk; 11am–2pm partly sunny and humid, 75–82°F, low-to-medium risk. The confirmed ride is 10am–2pm — meaning it ends right as the day would be heading into the 2–5pm scattered-storm window, not during it. The weather risk you were shown mostly doesn't apply to this specific ride.

What you told me about this family

They don't like summer, prefer winter, aren't "people persons," and don't like bugs. An open-air bench car along a river through forest in late July — even in a 68–82°F window — means humidity, sun through open sides, and real insect exposure with no way to close it out. Premier is enclosed, quiet specifically because it's 18+, and matches a family that would rather have their own space than share an open platform with strangers.

Recommendation: keep Premier for the ride, get the nature immersion during the 2-hour McCaysville layover instead. Walk along the river, sit outside for lunch, let the forest and the town do the "hear the outdoors" thing you're after — then retreat to a climate-controlled, bug-free, quiet car for the actual 4 hours of moving time. This also directly helps Mom, who's running on a short night — reclining in a cool, quiet car is a real chance for her to sleep on the ride, which an open-air bench seat doesn't offer.
